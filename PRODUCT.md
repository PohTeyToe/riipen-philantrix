# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Two readers, in this order.

1. Trevin Noble and the Philantrix team, opening a link on a laptop between calls. They sell impact reporting to nonprofits and know the pain better than anyone: "Every funder wants a different format." They'll give the page about a minute and want to see their own problem solved on data shaped like theirs (Workstreams, Groups, Participants, Engagements, Tracked Goals). Their developer, or anyone they forward it to, will look closer at how it's built.
2. Program staff at a small nonprofit at quarter end, the people Philantrix sells to: one quarter of attendance records, several funders who each publish their own reporting form, and gaps in the data nobody noticed until the report was due.

## Product Purpose

Turn a nonprofit's program records into each funder's own report. An agent reads a funder's published reporting form, maps every question to the records, and leaves only what the records can't answer for a person. Code counts every number from the records, live, and every number opens to the rows behind it. A data check finds gaps first and asks only what the records can't settle. Names never reach funder copy, and counts that could identify someone are hidden.

Success: in under a minute, a viewer sees one set of records fill three real funders' forms, watches the recorded agent run that mapped them, answers a gap or edits a record and sees the numbers move, and clicks a number to see exactly which records made it.

## Brand Personality

Precise, quick, candid. It should feel like a screen Philantrix could ship: their canvas, their mahogany, their Lora titles, their orange for what needs a person. Confidence comes from visible machinery and correct numbers, not adjectives. The agent is shown as what it is: a recorded run with real timings, tool calls, checks and rejections.

## Anti-references

- AI-demo landing pages: a beige serif essay, gradient blobs, emoji, badge rows, a centered marketing hero, "Try this first" numbered walkthroughs, a pitch above the product.
- Fake agent theatre: spinners, typewriter "thinking" text, or step timelines invented for plain code. Every step shown happened, with its recorded or measured time.
- Hero-metric dashboards: a wall of identical KPI cards with tiny grey captions.
- Walls of small grey explanatory text. If a thing needs explaining, the interface is wrong or the text goes behind "How it works".

## Design Principles

1. The product is the first screen: the funder's report, the run that built it, and the records it came from.
2. Show the machinery, and make each part earn its place by what it does for the reader.
3. Every number has a source. Click it, see the rows, see them light up in the records.
4. Only real work. The agent run is a replay of a recorded run, labeled with its date. Deterministic code runs live in the browser, with its measured time.
5. A person decides only what the records can't. Those fields are marked, answerable in place, and never filled by a guess.
6. Privacy is visible. The guard's effect shows on the report itself.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Every control reachable and usable by keyboard, with visible focus. Status never relies on colour alone (a word or shape goes with it). Motion respects prefers-reduced-motion, and the replay has a still, complete state. Works at 375px wide with no sideways page scroll.
